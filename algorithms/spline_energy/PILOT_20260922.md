# Ant pilot: energy Bellman regression, 2026-09-22

**Outcome: no demonstrated resolution of the Ant failure.** A modest difference
in negative return on one training seed does not establish an improvement.
The proposed loss increased sampled TD error and pre-clipping gradient norms.
The default trainer remains the original Huber variant.

## Question and controlled change

Can a target-relative generalized-KL regression loss, derived from the GMM40
energy-fitting objective, improve Bellman fitting without changing the spline
architecture or one-forward sampler? See [THEORY.md](THEORY.md) for the precise
reweighting, assumptions, stochastic bias, and conditional bounds.

Baseline: frozen Spline Energy + Huber, Ant-v4, seed 0, first 100,000 steps of
the original 1M run. Modified: same settings, relative energy loss with eta=10,
kappa=4, 100,000 steps. Rank 16, 33 knots, alpha=.25, gamma=.99, EMA tau=.005,
Adam 3e-4, batch 256, warmup 5,000, gradient clip 10, and one update/step remain
fixed. Each evaluation uses the same ten reset/action seeds and the native
stochastic policy. Both use Gymnasium 0.29.1 and MuJoCo 2.3.7.

## Measurements

| Metric | Original Huber | Relative energy |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100k evaluation mean return | -61.623 | -34.088 |
| Within-evaluation episode standard deviation | 89.717 | 64.002 |
| Reward AUC / environment steps, 0–100k | -24.355 | -20.402 |
| 100k evaluation mean episode length | 341.8 | 487.7 |
| Mean sampled absolute TD error, last 10k | 2.478 | 4.195 |
| Mean pre-clipping gradient norm, last 10k | 152.051 | 1372.258 |

Training diagnostics average the ten 1k-step log samples in (90k,100k], each
from its own replay minibatch. Different learned policies create different
replay distributions; these metrics do not compare errors on a common test set.
The table's standard deviation is across evaluation episodes, not training
seeds. No statistical superiority or 1M performance claim is supported.

The scalar loss passed its derivative, finite-tail and contextual gradient
checks, and training completed without a numeric failure. This does not imply
that the loss improves neural optimization: its asymmetric emphasis on positive
TD residuals can amplify parameter gradients despite the bounded scalar
curvature. The pilot is consistent with that concern; it does not identify the
unique cause of the original Ant failure.

## What this says about GMM40 transfer

The original GMM40 experiment had a fixed oracle density, uniform/policy
proposal sampling, exact proposal weights, two action dimensions, and rank
64 / 129 knots. The MuJoCo port instead bootstraps targets, trains under replay,
conditions on states, and uses rank 16 / 33 knots. Exact normalization is shared;
the learning problem and objective are different. This pilot only tests the
loss adaptation. It does not rule out coverage, target error, state-conditioned
optimization, or capacity as contributors. Do not replace the successful GMM40
reference or claim that this pilot has repaired MuJoCo learning.
